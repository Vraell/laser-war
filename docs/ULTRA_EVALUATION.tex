    \textbf{The central idea.}
    A positive score favors the player whose turn it is. A negative score favors
    the opponent. The score combines king safety, future laser access, permanent
    laser assignments, route speed, and the lasers' current impact.
  }%

\section*{1. The complete score}

For a non-terminal position, Ultra evaluates
\[
E =
S_{\mathrm{count}}
+ S_{\mathrm{assign}}
+ S_{\mathrm{route}}
+ S_{\mathrm{exposure}}.
\]

Every term is measured from the side to move. When the turn changes, the point
of view changes with it. This makes the score symmetric between Red and Blue.

\begin{center}
\rowcolors{2}{panel}{white}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{0.22\textwidth}X>{\raggedleft\arraybackslash}p{0.19\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Question being asked} & \textbf{Typical scale}\\
\midrule
Shield material & Who has more surviving cover around their king? & tens\\
Assignment control & Has a laser become permanently committed to one king? & thousands\\
Route control & Who can complete the important laser routes sooner? & hundreds to thousands\\
Exposure & Is a king on a live laser path right now? & hundreds\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

If the game is already over, these positional terms are ignored:
\[
E =
\begin{cases}
+\MateScore, & \text{the side to move is the winner},\\
-\MateScore, & \text{the opponent is the winner},\\
0, & \text{the result is a draw}.
\end{cases}
\]

\section*{2. King cover}

Each king begins with six shields in fixed positions: five adjacent shields
and one outer shield. Shields can be destroyed but never moved or created.
Consequently, the evaluation treats every surviving shield equally rather
than pretending that the formation can change shape.

Let \(N_{\mathrm{own}}\) and \(N_{\mathrm{opp}}\) be the surviving shield counts
around the two kings. Then
\[
S_{\mathrm{count}}
= \ShieldCountWeight\left(N_{\mathrm{own}}-N_{\mathrm{opp}}\right).
\]

Each extra nearby shield is therefore worth \(\ShieldCountWeight\) points.

\section*{3. Permanent laser assignment}

This decisive strategic feature was introduced in v0.13.4.

The rules require the two lasers to retain compatible future routes to
different kings. Suppose one laser can now reach only the Blue king. Because
the two target kings must differ, that laser is permanently assigned to Blue
and the other laser must serve the route to Red. Later mirror placements can
remove options, but they cannot restore a route already made impossible.

For each laser:
\[
a_\ell =
\begin{cases}
+1, & \text{it can reach only the opposing king},\\
-1, & \text{it can reach only the current player's king},\\
0, & \text{it can still reach both kings}.
\end{cases}
\]
The assignment score is
\[
S_{\mathrm{assign}}
= \AssignmentWeight\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}a_\ell.
\]

The large weight is intentional. A dedicated attack laser is not a small
positional preference: it permanently defines which side can build with that
laser and which king must absorb its pressure.

There is no separate reachability-count term. Because the rules require every
laser to reach at least one king, the difference between the number of lasers
that can reach each king is exactly \(\sum a_\ell\). Counting both would measure
the same fact twice.

\section*{4. Route control}

Reachability answers \emph{whether} a route exists. Route control estimates
\emph{how many new mirrors} are needed to complete it.

For each laser \(\ell\), let
\[
c_\ell(P)
= \min\!\left(\text{fewest new mirrors needed to reach king }P,\ \RouteLimit\right).
\]
An unreachable target is also assigned the capped value \(\RouteLimit\).

For the opposing king, sort the two costs:
\[
\alpha_1\leq\alpha_2.
\]
For the current player's king, sort them separately:
\[
\delta_1\leq\delta_2.
\]

The two race gaps are
\[
r=\delta_1-\alpha_1,
\qquad
q=\delta_2-\alpha_2.
\]
A positive \(r\) means the quickest attack on the opponent is closer than the
quickest danger to the current player's king. The reserve gap \(q\) compares
the second laser and therefore rewards control that is not dependent on a
single route.

Define the signed square
\[
\sigma(x)=x|x|.
\]
It preserves the sign of a lead while making large leads matter more than
small ones. For each laser, also define its individual control gap
\[
g_\ell=c_\ell(\mathrm{own})-c_\ell(\mathrm{opponent}).
\]

The complete route term is
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{route}}={}&
\RouteRaceLinear r
+\RouteReserveLinear q\\
&+\RouteRaceQuadratic\sigma(r)
+\RouteReserveQuadratic\sigma(q)
+\RoutePerLaserQuadratic\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sigma(g_\ell).
\end{aligned}
\]

\textbf{Why all three comparisons?}
The quickest route measures the immediate race, the second route measures
resilience, and the per-laser gaps preserve information about which physical
laser controls each side of the board.

\section*{5. Current laser exposure}

The board's present laser paths are evaluated after all existing mirrors:
\[
S_{\mathrm{exposure}}
=
\begin{cases}
+\ExposureWeight, & \text{only the opposing king is currently hit},\\
-\ExposureWeight, & \text{only the current player's king is currently hit},\\
0, & \text{neither king or both kings are hit}.
\end{cases}
\]

Both kings being hit is neutral in this term because the lasers fire
simultaneously. The actual game result, when a move is resolved, still takes
priority over every positional score.

\section*{6. Reading a score}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A score near zero means the measurable advantages are balanced; it
  does not guarantee a draw.
  \item A few hundred points usually indicate route or exposure pressure.
  \item A swing of roughly \(\AssignmentWeight\) points usually signals that a
  laser has become permanently dedicated to one king.
  \item Scores near \(\pm\MateScore\) represent a forced or completed win and
  dominate every positional consideration.
\end{itemize}

\colorbox{panel}{%
  \parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep\relax}{%
    \textbf{Example: the permanent-lane failure.}
    If both lasers initially reach both kings, the assignment term is zero.
    If a move makes the left laser able to reach only Blue, then from Red's
    point of view the assignment term becomes \(+\AssignmentWeight\); from
    Blue's point of view it becomes \(-\AssignmentWeight\). This captures the
    irreversible strategic change even before the final mirror route is built.
  }%
}

\section*{7. Worked example}

Consider a position evaluated for Red:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Both sides have four nearby shields, so
  \(S_{\mathrm{count}}=25(4-4)=0\).
  \item One laser can reach only Blue and the other still reaches both kings,
  so \(S_{\mathrm{assign}}=1218(1+0)=1218\).
  \item The sorted attack costs are \((2,5)\), the sorted danger costs are
  \((4,7)\), and each physical laser has a control gap of \(2\).
  Consequently,
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  S_{\mathrm{route}}
  &=67(2)+42(2)+28\sigma(2)+18\sigma(2)
    +8\bigl(\sigma(2)+\sigma(2)\bigr)\\
  &=466.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  \item Neither king is currently exposed, so
  \(S_{\mathrm{exposure}}=0\).
\end{itemize}

The complete evaluation is therefore
\[
E=0+1218+466+0=\boxed{1684}.
\]
This is a large Red advantage. Most of it comes from the permanent assignment,
while the route race confirms that Red is also closer to converting that
structural advantage.
